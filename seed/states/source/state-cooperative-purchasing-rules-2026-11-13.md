# Fifty states split on cooperative purchasing

Most states let local governments and school districts buy through national cooperative contracts, but no two states grant that permission on the same terms. By this report's tally, roughly thirty states have an express statute or rule that lets at least one class of local buyer use another government's or a cooperative's contract in place of its own bid; the rest leave the question to general interlocal-cooperation law, home rule, and local purchasing ordinances. The conditions that recur are a competitively bid lead contract awarded by a public agency, a written agreement or governing-body resolution, and an exclusion for construction. Only **Alabama** (a published list of approved cooperatives) and **Mississippi** (a list of approved cooperative contracts, usable only by certified purchasing offices) run true state approval lists; **Arkansas, Delaware, Hawaii, New Jersey and Oklahoma** have narrower approval or registration mechanisms. Statewide formal-bid triggers for goods range from **$2,500** (Maine counties) to **$250,000** (Idaho and Oregon), and a large group of states set no statewide threshold for municipal goods at all. The 2025–2026 period moved many numbers: Texas doubled its thresholds to $100,000, Idaho went to $100,000/$250,000, Kentucky to $50,000, Connecticut's waiver cap to $35,000, and New York's piggyback statute was extended only to June 30, 2027. The research behind this report read many statutes through third-party mirrors and secondary summaries, so every entry below carries the verification flags from the underlying notes; flagged items are leads to confirm, not settled law.

## Three legal postures explain nearly every state

The fifty states sort into three groups. The first has an **express piggyback or cooperative statute** that reaches out-of-state lead agencies: Texas, Nevada, Arizona, Idaho, Tennessee, Virginia, North Carolina, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, New York, Ohio, Minnesota, South Dakota, Oklahoma, Arkansas, Washington, Oregon and others. In these states the statute itself says the cooperative purchase satisfies, or is exempt from, local bidding. The second group has express authority for **one class of buyer only**: school districts in Florida, Kansas, North Dakota, California, Montana and West Virginia; counties in Nebraska; special districts in Florida. The third group has **no express statute** for local buyers and relies on interlocal-cooperation acts and local policy: Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Georgia, Michigan, Iowa, Missouri, Wyoming, and, for municipalities and counties, Delaware, West Virginia, Kansas, North Dakota and California. In this third group the operative document is the local charter, ordinance or board policy, and where a state bid statute does apply (county construction in Kansas, school purchases in Wyoming and Vermont) it usually contains no cooperative exemption.

Construction is the fault line that cuts across all three groups. New York's appellate courts (2026), South Dakota's attorney general (2026), Ohio's attorney general (2019 and 2024), California's attorney general (2006) and statutes or state guidance in New Jersey, Virginia, North Carolina, Tennessee, Nevada, Oregon, Massachusetts and Oklahoma all restrict or bar cooperative contracts for public works. A second recurring test is whether the awarding entity is itself a public body: New York, New Jersey, Tennessee, Washington and Kansas (schools) all turn on that point, which matters for cooperatives whose contracts are awarded by a nonprofit administrator.

### How to read the flags

The notes behind this report used a fetch tool that returned summaries with short quotations, so subsection lettering should be re-checked before any provision is quoted in a legal document. Four flags appear in the entries. **[mirror]** means the statute was read on Justia, FindLaw, Counsel Stack, public.law or a similar third-party site rather than the official code, with the mirror's date where the notes gave one. **[secondary]** means the point rests on a league, law-firm, association or cooperative-vendor source and was not checked against statute text. **[unverified]** means the notes could not confirm the point in any fetched source. **[currency unconfirmed]** means the text read may have been amended since. Unflagged statements were read on an official legislature or agency site. Nothing here is legal advice.

## Northeast: New York's piggyback law now sunsets in June 2027

Three northeastern states have an express statute for local use of other governments' contracts (New York, Massachusetts, Connecticut), Rhode Island has express authority only for state agreements and school consortia, and Maine, New Hampshire and Vermont have no general municipal bidding statute at all.

### Connecticut

**Authority.** CGS § 7-148v(b) lets a municipality, notwithstanding any statute, charter or ordinance, buy equipment, supplies, materials or services from a vendor holding a contract with other state governments, Connecticut political subdivisions, nonprofit organizations, or public purchasing consortia available through a regional educational service center (RESC) or regional council of governments (COG) **[mirror]** ([Justia § 7-148v](https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-7/chapter-98/section-7-148v/)). The grammar leaves open whether "available through a RESC or COG" qualifies only consortia or the whole list; on the stricter reading a national contract should be reached through a RESC or COG program. **Conditions.** The only statutory condition is that the purchase follow the underlying contract's terms; the text read shows no resolution, savings finding, dollar cap or contract-age limit. **Schools.** State-assisted school construction must be publicly bid, and a qualified bidder may be a cooperative contract offered through a RESC or COG, under CGS § 10-287(b)(1) **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2025; 2026 supplement changes not read]** ([FindLaw § 10-287](https://codes.findlaw.com/ct/title-10-education-and-culture/ct-gen-st-sect-10-287/)). Whether a board of education is a "municipality" for § 7-148v(b) was not confirmed. **List.** No state registry; RESCs and COGs are the named conduits. **Thresholds.** There is no statewide micro, quote or bid threshold for municipalities; a municipal ordinance may waive sealed bidding only up to **$35,000** (raised from $25,000 by P.A. 25-92; effective date not confirmed) **[mirror]**. State-assisted school construction is bid at **$10,000 and above**.

### Maine

**Authority.** No express piggyback statute and no general municipal bidding statute was found. Cooperative membership rests on the Interlocal Cooperation Act, 30-A M.R.S. §§ 2201–2203, which requires a written agreement plus governing-body ordinance or resolution **[secondary: vendor flyer dated 2020; not read on the legislature's site]** ([Inceptia Maine flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_Maine_0113120.pdf)). **Schools.** School units fall within the interlocal act **[secondary]**; school building construction over **$250,000** requires sealed bids under 5 M.R.S. § 1743-A, with no cooperative exception and a waiver only in unusual circumstances ([5 § 1743-A](https://www.legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/5/title5sec1743-A.html)). **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** Counties must bid purchases over **$2,500**, may take procurements of $10,000 or less by oral proposal, and are exempt when buying through the State, under 30-A M.R.S. § 124 ([30-A § 124](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/30-A/title30-Asec124.html)). For cities and towns no state threshold was located; the notes treat "no state bidding law for municipalities" as probable but **[unverified]**. School goods thresholds are set by district policy **[secondary]**.

### Massachusetts

**Authority.** M.G.L. c. 30B § 22 lets a public procurement unit join a cooperative purchasing agreement with other public procurement units or external procurement activities, but for **supplies only**, under an agreement among participants and through full and open competition ([c. 30B § 22](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIII/Chapter30B/Section22)). The Inspector General's July 2026 manual confirms that contracts procured by in-state or out-of-state political subdivisions or federal or state agencies qualify if open to local jurisdictions; services cannot be bought this way, and construction is governed by c. 149 and c. 30 § 39M ([OIG Chapter 30B Manual 2026](https://maoig.gov/wp-content/uploads/Chapter-30B-Manual-2026.pdf)). **Conditions.** The OIG sets a five-point due-diligence checklist on the lead agency's advertising, purchase description, renewed competition, award rule and evaluation process. **Schools.** Same c. 30B framework. **List.** None; the burden sits with the local buyer. **Thresholds.** Under **$10,000**, sound business practices; **$10,000 to $50,000** (to **$100,000** for municipal and regional school districts), three written quotes; above that, sealed bids or proposals ([c. 30B § 4](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleIII/Chapter30B/Section4)). House 5131 would raise and equalize these ($25,000/$100,000) and extend § 22 to services; it was still pending in the latest source read (August 2026), so its status through November 2026 is **[currency unconfirmed]** ([OIG letter](https://www.mass.gov/doc/oig-letter-to-the-house-ways-and-means-committee-re-house-5131-the-municipal-empowerment-act/download); [MMA tracker](https://www.mma.org/overview-of-timely-state-and-federal-issues/)). The full building-construction tier structure under c. 149 was **[unverified]**; confirm against the [OIG procurement charts](https://www.mass.gov/doc/oig-charts-on-procurement-procedures-july-2025/download).

### New Hampshire

**Authority.** No express piggyback statute and no general bidding statute for towns, cities or school districts. The interlocal route is RSA 53-A:3 (agreement plus governing-body action) **[secondary: vendor flyer, 2020]** ([Inceptia NH flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_NewHampshire_011320.pdf)). The New Hampshire Municipal Association states that no state law requires competitive bidding for town purchases, in an article that carries its own out-of-date warning **[secondary]** ([NHMA](https://www.nhmunicipal.org/node/661)). **Schools.** Districts are public agencies under RSA 53-A **[secondary]**. SB 580 (2026), which would have created a state school cooperative purchasing program, was killed in the House on April 23, 2026 and is not law **[secondary: bill trackers]** ([LegiScan SB 580](https://legiscan.com/NH/bill/SB580/)). **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** The only state-set figure is for counties: competitive bidding for equipment or materials over **$10,000**, waivable by unanimous recorded commissioner vote ([RSA 28:8](https://www.gc.nh.gov/rsa/html/II/28/28-8.htm)). Towns, cities and school districts follow local policy.

### Rhode Island

**Authority.** Municipal procurement runs under R.I.G.L. ch. 45-55, which has no cooperative-purchasing section ([ch. 45-55 index](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE45/45-55/INDEX.htm)). Express authority exists only for state master price agreements: any municipality or regional school district may participate if the contractor extends the terms ([§ 37-2-56](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE37/37-2/37-2-56.htm)). That section mentions an "intergovernmental cooperative purchase contract" without defining it or addressing out-of-state cooperatives, so a city using a national co-op above the threshold stands on weaker statutory ground than in neighboring states; that conclusion is an inference in the notes, not a verified legal position. **Schools.** Purchases by school consortia formed under § 16-2-9.2 are exempt from all of ch. 45-55 ([§ 45-55-13.3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE45/45-55/45-55-13.3.htm)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Sealed bidding applies above statutory bases of **$10,000 (construction)** and **$5,000 (other)**, adjusted annually by the Boston CPI; below them, local small-purchase rules govern with no state quote count ([§ 45-55-9](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE45/45-55/45-55-9.htm)). The current CPI-adjusted dollar figures were not found. 2026 H 7224 would raise the bases to $25,000 and $10,000; enactment was not confirmed **[currency unconfirmed]** ([H 7224](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/BillText26/HouseText26/H7224.htm)).

### Vermont

**Authority.** Municipalities have no state bidding mandate; the legislative body sets its own tiers, so cooperative use is a local-policy question **[secondary: VLCT model policy, Dec. 2025]** ([VLCT](https://www.vlct.org/node/346)). The Commissioner of Buildings and General Services may act as a political subdivision's purchasing agent ([29 V.S.A. § 902](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/29/049/00902)). **Schools.** School boards must publicly advertise or invite three or more bids above **$40,000**; the listed exemptions cover joint bidding with other districts and purchases through the Commissioner, **not national cooperatives**, and the Secretary of Education may grant waivers ([16 V.S.A. § 559](https://legislature.vermont.gov/statutes/section/16/009/00559)). A district buying more than $40,000 through a national co-op therefore needs a waiver, its own three-bid invitation, or a State contract. School construction over $2,000,000 adds prequalification and 60-day advertising. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Municipal: locally set. Schools: $40,000, with no state micro or quote tier below it. The 2026 amendment (Act 164) was technical and left $40,000 unchanged.

### New York

**Authority.** General Municipal Law § 103(16) lets political subdivisions and districts buy apparatus, materials, equipment and supplies, and related installation, maintenance or repair services, through a contract let by another government entity; the Senate site labels it repealed June 30, 2027 ([GML § 103](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/103)). The one-year extension was signed June 29, 2026 **[secondary: law-firm alert, consistent with the official sunset label]** ([Hodgson Russ](https://www.hodgsonruss.com/newsroom/publications/new-york-piggybacking-provision-extended-for-another-year)). **Conditions.** The State Comptroller sets three prerequisites: the contract was let by the United States, a state, or a political subdivision or district (private or nonprofit awards do not qualify); it was made available to other governments in the contract itself; and it was let to the lowest responsible bidder or on best value in a manner consistent with § 103 ([OSC bulletin](https://www.osc.ny.gov/localgov/pubs/piggybackinglaw.pdf)). No statutory resolution, membership agreement or savings finding applies, though best-value awards require prior local authorization. **Public works are excluded**, a limit the Comptroller ties to the May 2026 appellate ruling in *Lynch v. Maine-Endwell*. **Schools.** School districts and BOCES fall under the same section. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Sealed bids for purchase contracts over **$20,000** and public work over **$35,000**; below those, each entity's own procurement policy under GML § 104-b governs, with no statutory micro or quote tier. State (OGS) contracts and federal schedules are available under GML § 104 ([GML § 104](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GMU/104)).

## Mid-Atlantic: New Jersey and North Carolina attach the heaviest paperwork

Five of these seven states have express statutes with out-of-state reach. Delaware and West Virginia leave local governments on general or charter authority.

### New Jersey

**Authority.** N.J.S.A. 52:34-6.2(b)(3) lets a contracting unit (counties, municipalities, school districts, fire districts, authorities) use a nationally recognized cooperative agreement developed through competitive bidding **[mirror: Justia 2019 codification; later amendment not ruled out]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/2019/title-52/section-52-34-6-2/)). **Conditions.** The statute requires a determination of cost savings. Local Finance Notice 2012-10 adds, as guidance: goods and services only, not public works; a lead contract awarded by a public unit through advertised open bidding that included New Jersey; a documented savings analysis; and, above the bid threshold, a governing-body resolution and a published notice of intent with at least 10 days' notice ([LFN 2012-10](https://www.trentonnj.org/DocumentCenter/View/254/Use-of-National-Coop-PDF)). **Schools.** Covered by the same statute. **List.** In-state cooperative pricing systems must be registered with and approved by the Division of Local Government Services; national cooperatives are not registered or pre-approved ([DLGS](https://www.nj.gov/dca/dlgs/programs/NJ_LAPL.shtml)). **Thresholds (effective July 1, 2025).** Formal bids at **$53,000** with a Qualified Purchasing Agent; **$17,500** for local units without one; **$39,000** for school districts without one. Quotes (two, if practicable) are required from 15% of the bid threshold: $7,950, $2,625 and $5,850 respectively; below that no quotes are required ([LFN 2025-08](https://nj.gov/dca/dlgs/lfns/2025/2025-08.pdf); [N.J.S.A. 40A:11-6.1, mirror](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/title-40a/section-40a-11-6-1/)).

### Pennsylvania

**Authority.** 62 Pa.C.S. § 1902 lets a public procurement unit join a cooperative purchasing agreement for supplies, services or construction with other units or external procurement activities; § 1901 defines local units to include political subdivisions and external activities to include out-of-state buying organizations **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2026]** ([FindLaw § 1902](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-62-pacsa-procurement/pa-csa-sect-62-1902/); [§ 1901](https://codes.findlaw.com/pa/title-62-pacsa-procurement/pa-csa-sect-62-1901/)). **Conditions.** Only an agreement between participants; no resolution, notice or savings finding in the statute. Other laws still bind construction: prevailing wage, steel procurement, bonding and, for schools, separate prime contracts **[secondary]** ([Fox Rothschild](https://production.jdsupra.com/legalnews/purchasing-co-ops-procurement-law-53445)). **Schools.** Covered as political subdivisions. **List.** None; the state program is COSTARS. **Thresholds (2026).** Below **$13,200**, nothing required; **$13,200 to $24,500**, three written or telephonic quotes; over **$24,500**, advertised bids. These figures come from association notices; the Pennsylvania Bulletin notice itself could not be fetched **[secondary]** ([PSAB](https://www.boroughs.org/fullnotice.php?id=979); [PSATS](https://www.psats.org/2026-bid-limits-published-early/)). The 2027 amounts had not been found as of the research date.

### Delaware

**Authority.** The state procurement code (29 Del. C. ch. 69) excludes local government units from "agency," so municipalities and counties have no express piggyback statute; municipal authority rests on individual charters **[unverified]** ([29 Del. C. ch. 69, subch. I](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title29/c069/sc01/index.html)). State contracts are open to local governments. **Schools.** School districts are agencies under ch. 69. Section 6933 lets the State procurement Section, with its director's written approval, join cooperative agreements for materiel or nonprofessional services with units in Delaware or another state, and grant temporary approval to another agency ([§ 6933](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title29/c069/sc03/)). That a district needs this approval to use a national co-op directly is the notes' inference, not a stated agency position. **List.** None; control is case-by-case written approval. **Thresholds (state agencies and school districts).** Materiel and nonprofessional services: under **$50,000** open market; **$50,000–$99,999.99** three written quotes; **$100,000+** formal bid. Public works: $150,000 and $250,000 ([MyMarketplace](https://mymarketplace.delaware.gov/agencies/spp.shtml)). Counties must invite sealed proposals above **$50,000** under 9 Del. C. § 314, with Kent and New Castle variations **[mirror, 2021; later amendments not reviewed]** ([Justia § 314](https://law.justia.com/codes/delaware/2021/title-9/chapter-3/subchapter-i/section-314/)). Municipal thresholds are set by charter **[unverified]**.

### Maryland

**Authority.** State Finance and Procurement § 13-110(e) lets a local entity (counties, municipal corporations, boards of education and others) join an existing intergovernmental cooperative purchasing agreement if its **governing body determines** that participation saves cost or administrative burden or furthers other policy goals ([SF&P § 13-110](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/gsf/13-110.pdf)). The notes caution that subsection (e)'s wording should be read in full before relying on it for out-of-state contracts. **Schools.** Education § 5-112(a)(3) exempts a county board's participation in contracts for **goods or commodities** awarded by other public agencies or intergovernmental purchasing organizations, if the lead agency followed public bidding ([Educ. § 5-112](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Statute_Web/ged/5-112.pdf)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** School systems advertise bids above **$100,000**; an older statute file read $25,000 and the date of the change was not confirmed. Commission counties bid above **$15,000** under Local Government § 12-301 **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2025]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/md/local-government/md-code-local-govt-sect-12-301/)); St. Mary's County is at $50,000 since October 1, 2025 ([Ch. 781](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2025RS/Chapters_noln/CH_781_hb0490t.pdf)). Charter counties, code counties and municipalities set their own thresholds **[unverified by jurisdiction]**; no statewide quote tier was found.

### Virginia

**Authority.** Va. Code § 2.2-4304(B) lets any public body (counties, cities, towns, school boards) buy from another public body's contract, including out-of-state, if the original solicitation stated it was a cooperative procurement on behalf of other public bodies ([§ 2.2-4304](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/2.2-4304/)). **Conditions.** Architectural and engineering contracts are excluded, as is construction except artificial turf and track, stream restoration, stormwater management, playground installation and, from July 1, 2026, non-structural roofing work on existing facilities. No resolution, notice or savings finding is required. Localities that have adopted alternative procurement policies follow those policies. **Schools.** Same statute. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** A public body may adopt written small-purchase procedures up to **$200,000** for goods and nonprofessional services, **$300,000** for non-transportation construction and **$80,000** for professional services; above those, sealed bidding or competitive negotiation. The statute sets no micro-purchase floor or quote count for local bodies ([§ 2.2-4303](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/2.2-4303/)).

### West Virginia

**Authority.** No express local piggyback statute. Local governments rely on the interlocal statute, W. Va. Code § 8-23-3 **[secondary: vendor flyer; official text not fetched]** ([flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_WestVirginia_021920.pdf)), and on local bidding rules: municipalities decide by ordinance whether bidding is required ([§ 8-12-10](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/8-12-10/)) and county commissions adopt their own procedures ([§ 7-1-11](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/7-1-11/)). **Schools.** State Board Policy 8200 permits purchases from "a legitimate government purchasing cooperative" whose bidding meets the policy, with justification on file **[mirror: 2012 text]** ([Policy 8200](https://regulations.justia.com/resources/v2024100900/wv/admin/2024/126-202-6.pdf)). The Department of Education's 2025 guidance says construction may not be procured directly from a cooperative contract ([WVDE slides](https://wvde.us/sites/default/files/2025-07/8200%20Revision..7.8.25.pdf)). **List.** No registry; Policy 8200 names example cooperatives. **Thresholds.** Counties: open market at **$15,000** or less, competitive bids above. Municipalities: set by ordinance. Construction for all local bodies and school boards: bids above **$50,000** ([§ 5-22-1](https://code.wvlegislature.gov/5-22-1/)). School boards under the 2012 Policy 8200 text: under $5,000 no bids; $5,000–$10,000 three verbal quotes; $10,000–$25,000 three written bids; $25,000–$50,000 advertisement; $50,000+ sealed bids. A pending revision described in July 2025 would set the competitive threshold at $10,000; adoption was not confirmed, so the school tiers are **[currency unconfirmed]**.

### North Carolina

**Authority.** G.S. 143-129(e)(3) exempts purchases through a competitively obtained group purchasing program serving two or more public agencies, and G.S. 143-129(g) lets a unit piggyback on a contract another public agency (federal, any state, or any state's subdivisions) formally bid **within the previous 12 months** ([G.S. 143-129](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_143/GS_143-129.html)). **Conditions.** Group purchasing needs no board action but is unavailable for construction or repair except HVAC systems within a $2,000,000 labor cap. Piggybacking requires governing-board approval at a regular meeting at least 10 days after published notice, and the same or better prices and terms. **Schools.** Same rules via G.S. 115C-522 ([G.S. 115C-522](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_115C/GS_115C-522.html)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Purchases: no statutory method under **$30,000**; informal bids from $30,000 to under **$90,000**; formal bids at $90,000. Construction or repair: informal from $30,000; formal at **$500,000** ([G.S. 143-131](https://ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_143/GS_143-131.html)). No quote count is specified. Two recent session laws amended § 143-129; the notes did not confirm what they changed, though current text still shows these figures.

## Southeast: Alabama and Mississippi run the only approved lists

The Southeast holds both extremes: Alabama and Mississippi gate cooperative purchasing through state approval, while Georgia and Florida leave cities and counties to home rule.

### South Carolina

**Authority.** S.C. Code § 11-35-4810 lets any public procurement unit join a cooperative purchasing agreement for supplies, services, information technology or construction; § 11-35-4610 defines local units as any political subdivision and reaches out-of-state buying organizations as "external procurement activities" **[mirror]** ([§ 11-35-4810](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/sc/35/11-35-4810); [§ 11-35-4610](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/sc/35/11-35-4610)). **Conditions.** An agreement among participants; multistate contracts must be awarded through full and open competition substantially equivalent to the state code. Whether the further state-level conditions in subsections (2)–(4) bind local governments operating under their own ordinances is not settled by the text read. **Schools.** All districts are political subdivisions; those spending more than $75 million a year fall under the state code or an approved substantially similar code ([SC Procurement Services](https://www.procurement.sc.gov/polsub/school-districts); [§ 11-35-5340, mirror](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/sc/35/11-35-5340)). **List.** None; the only approval mechanism is the Materials Management Officer's written approval of a nonprofit entity made up of public procurement units. **Thresholds.** Counties, municipalities and special purpose districts set their own by ordinance under § 11-35-50 **[mirror, 2017 code year]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/south-carolina/2017/title-11/chapter-35/section-11-35-50/)). The state code tiers, which bind large school districts, are **$10,000** (no quotes), **$25,000** (three written quotes) and **$100,000** (advertised written solicitation), with formal source selection above **[mirror; post-2019 amendments not ruled out]** ([§ 11-35-1550](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/sc/35/11-35-1550)).

### Georgia

**Authority.** No express local piggyback statute. Authority rests on the constitutional intergovernmental-contracts clause (Art. IX, § III, ¶ I) and O.C.G.A. § 36-69A-4, which lets Georgia agencies act jointly with other states' agencies after governing-body action by resolution; neither mentions purchasing **[secondary: vendor flyer quoting the provisions]** ([Inceptia Georgia flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_Georgia_011320.pdf)). State contracts are open to local governments ([DOAS](https://doas.ga.gov/state-purchasing/information-for-local-governments-and-municipalities)). **Schools.** Named in the same constitutional clause **[secondary]**; the school purchasing article contains no cooperative section **[mirror]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/georgia/title-20/chapter-2/article-10)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** No general state statute sets micro, quote or bid thresholds for local goods and services; that point is **[unverified]** and rests on indirect support. Bid opportunities of **$100,000 or more** must be posted on the Georgia Procurement Registry ([DOAS memo](https://doas.ga.gov/assets/State%20Purchasing/Communications/GPR%20for%20Local%20Govt%20HB%20322%20Memo%20May%2022%202019.doc.pdf)). Public works construction is governed by O.C.G.A. Title 36, ch. 91 regardless of procurement vehicle; the threshold read in statute was $100,000 **[mirror, 2019 code]**, and HB 137 reportedly raised it, along with road and school thresholds, to **$250,000** effective July 1, 2025 **[secondary: one law-firm summary; enrolled bill not read]** ([Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/insights/georgia-construction-law-2025-legislative-year-in-review)).

### Florida

**Authority.** Counties and municipalities have no general state bid statute for goods and services; authority comes from home rule, local ordinance or special act, a basis the notes mark **[unverified]** (Hillsborough County's policy is the illustration) ([Hillsborough County](https://hcfl.gov/businesses/doing-business-with-hillsborough/vendors/procurement-policy/chapter-10-cooperative-procurement-and-use-of-other-government-agency-contracts)). Special districts may buy from the competitively procured agreements of other special districts, municipalities or counties under § 189.053, an in-state list **[mirror, through Fall 2025]** ([§ 189.053](https://florida.public.law/statutes/fla._stat._189.053)). **Schools.** State Board Rule 6A-1.012(6) lets district boards buy, in lieu of soliciting three sources, from contracts of other governmental agencies, including "the public or governmental agencies of any state," when the vendor extends the same terms and the purchase is to the board's economic advantage ([Rule 6A-1.012](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-6A-1-012)). A contract awarded by a non-governmental nonprofit does not obviously fit the rule's list. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** School districts solicit three or more sources above **$50,000**; below that, board policy. Local construction must be competitively awarded above **$300,000** (**$75,000** electrical), base amounts indexed to a construction cost index; the current indexed figures were not found ([§ 255.20](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0255/Sections/0255.20.html)). City and county goods thresholds are local **[unverified]**.

### Alabama

**Authority.** Ala. Code § 41-16-51(a)(16) exempts county and municipal purchases of goods or services made through a national or regional governmental purchasing cooperative **[mirror]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/alabama/title-41/chapter-16/article-3/section-41-16-51)); § 16-13B-2(a)(13) does the same for boards of education ([2026 bill reprint](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SL6QB75-1.pdf)). **Conditions.** The goods or services must come from a competitive bid process by a governmental entity that the Department of Examiners of Public Accounts has approved; they must be unavailable on, or no more expensive than, the state purchasing program; and the purchase must go through an Alabama-licensed vendor if one exists. Public works are outside the exemption except HVAC units and, from June 1, 2026, playground equipment ([SB196 enrolled](https://alison.legislature.state.al.us/files/pdf/SearchableInstruments/2026RS/SB196-enr.pdf)). **List.** Yes. The Examiners' list as read on November 13, 2026 shows Sourcewell, NCPA, H-GAC, E&I, TIPS, NPP, BuyBoard, NASPO, OMNIA Partners and a PEPPM Apple contract, with separate HVAC approvals ([Purchasing Co-ops list](https://beta.alaleg.gov/epa-purchasing-coops)). **Thresholds.** Sealed bids at **$30,000** for counties and municipalities **[mirror]** ([§ 41-16-50](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/al/41-16-50)), **$40,000** for boards of education **[mirror]** ([§ 16-13B-1](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/al/16-13B-1)), and above **$100,000** for public works. CPI indexing does not begin until October 1, 2027. That no quote tier applies below the bid thresholds is **[unverified]**.

### Mississippi

**Authority.** Miss. Code § 31-7-13(m)(xxix) exempts purchases by **certified purchasing offices** of governing authorities under cooperative purchasing agreements **previously approved** by the state Office of Purchasing and Travel, if the agreement is in the entity's best interest and the original notice made it available to other governments ([SB2298 reprint](https://billstatus.ls.state.ms.us/documents/2025/pdf/SB/2200-2299/SB2298SG.pdf)). **Conditions.** Certification requires that at least half the office's purchasing agents hold a recognized purchasing credential; only a certified office may submit a contract for approval ([12 Miss. Code R. 6-2.104](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/mississippi/12-Miss-Code-R-SS-6-2-104)). An entity without a certified office cannot use the route at all. **Schools.** That districts are "governing authorities" under § 31-7-1 is **[unverified]**. **List.** Yes, at the contract level; the list itself was not read. **Thresholds.** Up to **$5,000**, no bids; over $5,000 to **$75,000**, two competitive written bids; over $75,000, advertised bids for two consecutive weeks. A 2026 bill to raise the $5,000 floor died; several other 2026 bills touching the section could not be retrieved, so the tiers are **[currency unconfirmed]** past mid-2025.

### Tennessee

**Authority.** T.C.A. § 12-3-1205(b) lets local governments join cooperative purchasing with out-of-state governmental entities to the extent the other state's law permits, where the goods were competitively bid and "advertised, evaluated, and awarded by a governmental entity" **[mirror, as of Jan. 2, 2024; later amendments not ruled out]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/tn/title-12-public-property-printing-and-contracts/tn-code-sect-12-3-1205/)). **Conditions.** A governing-body **resolution accepting the master agreement**, and retained documentation of the lead entity's compliance. Excluded: most new motor vehicles; construction, engineering and architectural services and construction materials (maintenance and repair of existing facilities excepted); and fuel. **Schools.** Local education agencies are named in the threshold statute and in the construction carve-out, but no source read confirms expressly that they may use subsection (b). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** The governing body sets the sealed-bid threshold by resolution or ordinance, capped at **$50,000** with centralized purchasing and a full-time agent, otherwise **$25,000**; three written quotes are required above 40% of the adopted threshold **[mirror]** ([§ 12-3-1212](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/tn/12-3-1212); [UT CTAS](https://ctas.tennessee.edu/eli/sealed-competitive-bids-and-public-advertising-threshold)).

### Kentucky

**Authority.** KRS 45A.300 lets any public purchasing unit join a cooperative purchasing agreement for supplies, services or construction and acquire from another unit "or foreign purchasing activity" ([KRS 45A.300](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57792)). The local Model Procurement Code provision, KRS 45A.420, covers only agreements with other Kentucky local public agencies, so national co-op use leans on 45A.300; whether 45A.300 applies to agencies that have not adopted the local code was not resolved. **Conditions.** An agreement between participants. **Schools.** Districts may buy supplies and equipment outside bidding if they meet the specifications of state, federal, local or cooperative contracts at a lower price and the finance officer certifies this beforehand ([KRS 424.260](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=57560)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Since July 15, 2026, both the small-purchase ceiling under the local code and the newspaper-advertised bid threshold are **$50,000** (up from $40,000), rising by $10,000 every five years from 2030 ([2026 Ky. Acts ch. 39](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/acts/26RS/documents/0039.pdf)). State law fixes no quote count or micro level; written local small-purchase procedures do.

## Great Lakes: only Ohio and Minnesota exempt national co-ops by name

Ohio and Minnesota exempt purchases through a national association's program from local bidding. The other five states rest on broader or more ambiguous provisions.

### Ohio

**Authority.** R.C. 9.48 lets a political subdivision join a joint purchasing program run by or through a national or state association of political subdivisions it is eligible to join; such purchases are exempt from competitive selection ([R.C. 9.48](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-9.48)). **Conditions.** The underlying contract must have been awarded through a publicly solicited competitive process in Ohio or another state, and the route is closed once the subdivision has received its own bids. No resolution is required. Attorney General opinions hold that construction services are outside R.C. 9.48 while installation, maintenance and repair qualify **[secondary]** ([Bricker Graydon](https://www.bricker.com/insights/publications/new-guidance-for-joint-purchasing-programs-under-r-c-9-48-sets-new-allowances)). **Schools.** Covered as political subdivisions **[secondary]** ([OSBA](https://www.ohioschoolboards.org/sites/default/files/OSBACompetitiveBiddingFactSheet.pdf)); state contracts are open by resolution under R.C. 125.04. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** The uniform bid amount under R.C. 9.17 was $75,000 through 2024 and rises 3% a year; the 2025 figure was **$77,250** ([Auditor Bulletin 2025-009](https://ohioauditor.gov/publications/bulletins/2025/2025-009.pdf)). **The 2026 figure was not confirmed** from an official source; arithmetic gives $79,567.50. Which entities use the 9.17 amount is **[secondary]**. No statewide quote tier was found.

### Michigan

**Authority.** No state statute requires competitive bidding on municipal contracts, so cooperative use turns on charter, ordinance or policy **[secondary]** ([MML Handbook](https://mml.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/HMO-CH-20-Finance-Purchasing.pdf)). The state's extended purchasing program is open to local units ([MCL 18.1263](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-18-1263.pdf)). **Schools.** Districts need not bid items bought through the state cooperative bulk purchasing program; no comparable exemption for national cooperatives appears, and whether a national co-op's solicitation satisfies the board's bid duty is **[unverified]** ([MCL 380.1274](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-380-1274.pdf)). School construction has no cooperative exception. **List.** State programs only. **Thresholds.** Municipalities and counties: local. Schools: the CPI-adjusted bid threshold is **$32,306 for fiscal 2026–27** **[secondary]** ([Clark Hill](https://www.clarkhill.com/news-events/news/michigan-school-bidding-threshold-2026-27/)); no statutory quote tier below it.

### Indiana

**Authority.** No express piggyback statute. IC 5-22-4-7 lets governmental bodies form a cooperative purchasing organization by interlocal agreement **[mirror]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/indiana/title-5/article-22/chapter-4/section-5-22-4-7/)), and IC 5-22-10-15 is a special-purchase method for contracts with a federal or state agency, whose text was not retrieved, so its reach to other states' contracts is unconfirmed ([SBOA bulletin](https://www.in.gov/sboa/files/Special%20Districts%20June%202026%20Bulletin_final.pdf)). **Schools.** Same tiers and special-purchase list. **List.** None found. **Thresholds (supplies).** Under **$50,000**, the agency's small-purchase policy; **$50,000 to $150,000**, quotes from at least three persons; **$150,000 or more**, sealed bids. Public works figures conflict between two official training decks ($150,000 in 2023; $300,000 in 2025) and the amending act was not confirmed, so the public works bid threshold is **unresolved** ([SBOA 2025](https://www.in.gov/sboa/files/IASBO-Purchasing-Seminar-4-10-25-1THJW.pdf)).

### Illinois

**Authority.** The Governmental Joint Purchasing Act lets governmental units buy jointly by competitive solicitation ([30 ILCS 525/2](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/003005250K2.htm)), but its definition of governmental unit does not mention other states, so the Act does not plainly reach an out-of-state lead agency **[mirror]** ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/codes/illinois/chapter-30/act-30-ilcs-525/)). Local units fall back on home rule and the Intergovernmental Cooperation Act **[unverified: not read]**. **Schools.** The School Code exempts "contracts for goods or services procured from another governmental agency" **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2025]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-105-schools/il-st-sect-105-5-10-20-21/)); the school boards association tells members to get an attorney opinion before buying above the threshold through its sponsored co-op **[secondary]** ([IASB](https://www.iasb.com/services/sponsored-programs/national-purchasing-co-op)). **List.** State education purchasing contracts only. **Thresholds.** Schools: bids over **$35,000** **[mirror; 2025–2026 changes not checked]**. Municipalities under 500,000: bids for public works over **$25,000**, with goods set by local ordinance **[mirror; currency unconfirmed]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/il/chapter-65-municipalities/il-st-sect-65-5-8-9-1/)). Counties under 2,000,000: advertised bids over **$30,000** ([55 ILCS 5/5-1022](https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/fulltext.asp?DocName=005500050K5-1022)). No quote tier found.

### Wisconsin

**Authority.** Wis. Stat. § 66.0131(2) lets any local governmental unit "make purchases from another unit of government, including the state or federal government, without the intervention of bids" ([§ 66.0131](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/66.0131)). Whether that reaches a purchase from a private vendor under another government's contract is not answered by the text read. Formal cooperation agreements with other states' municipalities must be submitted to the attorney general ([§ 66.0303](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/66.0301)). **Schools.** Districts may make intergovernmental contracts; the common statement that Wisconsin has no general school bidding statute is **[unverified]**. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Cities: public construction over **$25,000** to the lowest responsible bidder, with notice from $5,000 **[mirror]** ([§ 62.15](https://law.justia.com/codes/wisconsin/chapter-62/section-62-15/)). Towns: the same $5,000 and $25,000 tiers, covering materials and supplies as well ([§ 60.47](https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/document/statutes/60.47)). County and village statutes were not read **[unverified]**. No quote tier found.

### Minnesota

**Authority.** Minn. Stat. § 471.345 subd. 15(b) lets a municipality (defined to include counties, towns, cities and school districts) buy supplies, materials or equipment without competitive bidding "through a national municipal association's purchasing alliance or cooperative created by a joint powers agreement" that buys from more than one source on competitive bids or quotations ([§ 471.345](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/471.345/pdf)). **Conditions.** Goods only; construction and services are not listed. For contracts over $25,000 the municipality must first consider the state's cooperative purchasing venture. **Schools.** Covered as municipalities. **List.** None; the state venture is a members-only program ([State Auditor](https://www.auditor.state.mn.us/media/jdlfty1p/2026-county-bidding-and-contract-requirements.pdf)). **Thresholds.** **$25,000 or less**, quotation or open market (two quotes when practicable if quotes are used); over $25,000 to **$175,000**, sealed bids or direct negotiation with two or more quotes; over $175,000, sealed bids. Not indexed; unchanged since 2018.

### Iowa

**Authority.** No state statute sets bidding procedures for city goods and services, so national co-op use for goods is a local-policy matter **[secondary]** ([Iowa League of Cities](https://iowaleague.org/?p=10330)). State master agreements are open to political subdivisions ([Iowa DAS](https://das.iowa.gov/state-employees/procurement/cooperative-purchasing-program)). No cooperative exemption was found for public improvements above the Chapter 26 thresholds. **Schools.** Eligible for state agreements; goods thresholds by board policy **[secondary]**. **List.** State program only. **Thresholds (public improvements only).** Cities in 2026 **[secondary]**: vertical bid **$206,000**; vertical quote $82,000 (under 50,000 population) or $114,000; horizontal bid $62,000 or $89,000. City figures rise on January 1, 2027 to $220,000, $87,000/$121,000 and $67,000/$97,000. County and school figures were confirmed only for 2025 (vertical bid $206,000; county vertical quote $153,000; county horizontal bid $115,000) **[secondary; 2026–2027 not separately confirmed]** ([Ahlers & Cooney](https://www.ahlerslaw.com/client-alerts/new-competitive-bid-and-quote-thresholds-effective-january-1-2025)).

## Plains and South Central: South Dakota's attorney general bars co-op construction

Oklahoma, Arkansas, South Dakota and Louisiana have express provisions; Missouri, Kansas, Nebraska and North Dakota split by class of buyer. Statute text in this region was returned as paraphrase, so sub-paragraph lettering needs re-checking.

### Missouri

**Authority.** No express, self-executing piggyback statute for cities or counties was found. Authority is assembled from RSMo 70.220 (general cooperation with other states and their subdivisions for "a common service") ([70.220](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=70.220)), RSMo 67.360 (use of state purchasing) and RSMo 34.046, which as amended effective August 28, 2026 lets the commissioner of administration use other governments' and governmental-nonprofit contracts and "allow political subdivisions to take part" ([34.046](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=34.046)). Section 34.046 does not give political subdivisions independent authority. **Conditions.** The underlying contract must have been established under the laws applicable to the establishing entity. The state Cooperative Procurement Program requires an approved membership application **[secondary]** ([Mo. OA](https://purch.oa.mo.gov/cooperative-procurement-program-faqs)). **Schools.** No school-specific statute; districts rely on the same route (inference). **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** Counties: bids not required on purchases of **$12,000 or less** from one vendor in any 90-day period; no quote tier ([50.660](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=50.660)). School construction: advertised sealed bids above **$50,000**, with no cooperative exemption ([177.086](https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=177.086)). Municipal and school goods thresholds: none found in state law, a gap **not confirmed from a primary source**.

### Kansas

**Authority.** No express piggyback statute for cities or counties; authority rests on the Interlocal Cooperation Act, K.S.A. 12-2901 et seq., including joint action with other states' agencies **[secondary: vendor flyer]** ([Inceptia Kansas flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_Kansas_111319.pdf)). **Schools.** K.S.A. 72-1151(b)(9)(C) exempts materials, goods or wares bought "under the same pricing provisions established in federal, national or other state contracts facilitated by a federal or local governmental entity or agency," if the vendor honors the price and the **board approves expenditures over $20,000** ([K.S.A. 72-1151](https://ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch72/072_011_0051.html)). The exemption does not cover construction. **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** Schools: sealed proposals above **$20,000**, no quote tier; a 2025 bill to raise this to $50,000 died. Counties: public letting for buildings, roads and bridges; the Revisor's page still showed $25,000 on the research date, but 2026 Session Laws ch. 64 raises it to **more than $100,000**, effective on publication in the statute book **[currency unconfirmed: effective date inferred, online code not yet updated]** ([2026 ch. 64](https://kssos.org/publications/sessionlaws/2026/Chapter-64-HB-2571.html)). County and city goods thresholds: none found, not confirmed.

### Nebraska

**Authority.** Counties have an express exemption: bidding is not required when the price was established by a cooperative purchasing agreement under a contract of another governmental entity or group, established under that entity's laws, per Neb. Rev. Stat. 23-3109 **[mirror]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/ne/chapter-23-county-government-and-officers/ne-rev-st-sect-23-3109/)). Cities and villages have only a narrow statute tied to federal and state pricing through a joint city-county purchasing agency ([18-1756](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=18-1756)); otherwise they rely on the Interlocal Cooperation Act, which requires governing-body action ([13-804](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=13-804)). **Schools.** No cooperative statute found; the school construction bid statute has no cooperative exception. **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** Counties (2025 to 2028): sealed bids at **$70,000**; three informal bids from **$15,000**; open market below **[mirror, dated Jan. 1, 2024, showing the scheduled tiers]** ([FindLaw 23-3108](https://codes.findlaw.com/ne/chapter-23-county-government-and-officers/ne-rev-st-sect-23-3108/)). First- and second-class cities and villages: advertised bids for improvements over **$90,000** (as amended effective July 18, 2026) ([16-321](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/statutes.php?statute=16-321)). School construction: **$136,000** per a September 2025 department notice **[secondary; effective date not stated]** ([NDE](https://www.education.ne.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/SchDistBidReqDoc_9-25-2025.pdf)). School goods: no state threshold found.

### South Dakota

**Authority.** One procurement code covers counties, municipalities and school districts. SDCL 5-18A-22(4) exempts purchases of supplies or services (not professional services) "from any active contract that has been awarded by any government entity by competitive sealed bids or competitive sealed proposals or from any contract that was competitively solicited and awarded within the previous twelve months" ([5-18A-22](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/5-18A-22.html)). **Conditions.** No membership agreement, resolution or savings finding is required; the Department of Legislative Audit recommends keeping the lead agency's specifications, publication affidavit and award minutes **[secondary]** ([DLA Bid Booklet](https://legislativeaudit.sd.gov/resources/Bid%20Booklet%202024.pdf)). **Schools.** Same exemption. Attorney General Official Opinion 26-03 (June 25, 2026) concluded that school districts may not enter construction contracts of $100,000 or more through out-of-state cooperatives without local bidding **[secondary: third-party reproduction; official PDF not read]** ([AG Op. 26-03](https://ezel.ai/ag-opinions/sd/26-03-school-district-construction-contracts-cooperative-purchasing)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Advertised bids for supplies or services of **$50,000 or more** and public improvements of **$100,000 or more** ([5-18A-14](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/5-18A-14.html)). Below $50,000, local governments and schools follow their own procedures; the state sets no quote count or micro level for them ([5-18A-11](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/5-18A-11.html)).

### North Dakota

**Authority.** No express piggyback statute for cities or counties. They rely on joint powers agreements, including with another state's political subdivisions, under NDCC 54-40.3-01 **[secondary: vendor flyer]** ([Inceptia ND flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_NorthDakota_021920.pdf)). The state cooperative statute, NDCC 54-44.4-13, does not name political subdivisions ([ch. 54-44.4](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t54c44-4.pdf)). **Schools.** NDCC 15.1-09-34 exempts from sealed bidding cooperative purchases with the state and "cooperative purchases made pursuant to a joint-powers agreement under chapter 54-40.3" **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2024; 2025 changes not checked]** ([FindLaw](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-15-1-elementary-and-secondary-education/nd-cent-code-sect-15-1-09-34/)). A co-op run by a private or nonprofit entity does not obviously fit. **List.** None found. **Thresholds.** Schools: sealed bids above **$50,000**, no quote tier **[mirror]**. Public improvements for all political subdivisions: **$250,000** ([ch. 48-01.2](https://ndlegis.gov/cencode/t48c01-2.pdf)). County road work: bids over $200,000, two quotes from $50,000; road machinery over $100,000 **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2024]** ([FindLaw 24-05-04](https://codes.findlaw.com/nd/title-24-highways-bridges-and-ferries/nd-cent-code-sect-24-05-04/)). City and general county goods: no state threshold found.

### Oklahoma

**Authority.** 61 O.S. § 139(B) lets local public procurement units join "cooperative or piggybacking purchasing agreements" for goods or services, including construction services, with public or external (out-of-state) procurement units, provided the purchase still satisfies applicable procurement law **[mirror, 2025 statutes]** ([Justia § 139](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-61/section-61-139/)). Counties are also exempt when using a NACo-sponsored program or "any other competitively bid nationwide purchasing program" under 19 O.S. § 1501(A)(3)(p) **[mirror]** ([Justia § 1501](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-19/section-19-1501/)). **Conditions.** A written agreement. For public construction over $100,000 the cooperative and contractor must have complied with Oklahoma's sealed-bid requirements **[mirror]** ([Justia § 103](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-61/section-61-103/)). **Schools.** § 139(A) authorizes cooperative purchasing by written agreement but excludes new construction; inter-district purchasing cooperatives need State Board of Education approval **[mirror]** ([70 O.S. § 5-117b](https://law.justia.com/codes/oklahoma/title-70/section-70-5-117b/)). **List.** No list of national co-ops. **Thresholds.** Public construction: sealed bids above **$100,000**; written bids or quotes at or below; negotiable under $10,000 **[mirror]**. Counties: bidding applies above **$25,000** per department **[mirror]**; any county quote tiers in § 1505 could not be read. Municipal and school goods: no state threshold found, not confirmed.

### Arkansas

**Authority.** Cities and towns may buy without bids if the governing body approves **by resolution** a purchase through the competitive procedures of the federal government, another state, or an association of governments or governmental agencies, under Ark. Code § 14-58-104(20) **[mirror]** ([Counsel Stack](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ar/14-58-104)). Counties have the same route with a quorum court resolution, plus a separate exemption for NACo and Association of Arkansas Counties programs, under § 14-22-106 **[mirror]** ([Counsel Stack](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ar/14-22-106)). A single out-of-state local government acting as lead agency must fit the "association" wording (inference). **Schools.** The school bidding exemptions contain no cooperative clause; authority rests on the state code's § 19-11-249, and the definition confirming school coverage was not read **[mirror]** ([§ 19-11-249](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ar/19-11-249)). **List.** The Office of State Procurement approves cooperative contracts individually **[secondary: undated policy]** ([OSP policy](https://www.transform.ar.gov/wp-content/uploads/City-County-Coop-Policy.pdf)). **Thresholds.** First-class cities and counties: bidding at the CPI-adjusted **$42,921** from January 1, 2025 (statutory base $35,000) ([DFA notice](https://dfa.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/01012025_adjustment_notice.pdf)). Schools: **$26,500** for 2025–26 ([ADE memo](https://media.ark.org/agri/Small-Purchase-Threshold-2526.pdf)); the 2026–27 figure in force now was **not found**. No quote tiers in statute.

### Louisiana

**Authority.** The Public Bid Law lets any public entity buy materials, supplies and equipment under the cooperative purchasing provisions of the Procurement Code ([R.S. 38:2212.1](https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=94917)), which authorize cooperative agreements with external procurement activities under an agreement among participants ([R.S. 39:1702](https://legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=96137)). Practice is narrower than the text suggests. The Legislative Auditor reports attorney general opinions holding that a public entity may not piggyback on a national cooperative contract not bid by a Louisiana public entity, and that in-state piggybacking under R.S. 38:321.1 requires use within one year of bid opening, written consent, a two-times quantity cap, and excludes public works **[secondary]** ([LLA FAQ](https://app.lla.la.gov/llala.nsf/87BD5C74CB17E03686257AB8006F37DE/$FILE/Public%20Bid%20Law%20FAQ.pdf)). The Auditor also reports that Act 665 of 2026 added subsection (Q), effective August 1, 2026, letting local governmental subdivisions use qualified group purchasing organizations, including out-of-state public ones; the legislature's online text read on the research date showed no subsection Q, so this is **[secondary; conflict unresolved]**. **Schools.** Districts may buy materials, equipment and supplies through a qualified group purchasing organization only if the price beats the state contract or the item is not on state contract (R.S. 38:2212.1(N)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Materials and supplies: advertised bids above **$60,000**; three quotes from **$30,000**; nothing required below. Public works: **$260,000** from February 1, 2026 **[secondary]**.

## Southwest and Mountain: Texas doubles thresholds to $100,000

Texas, Arizona and Nevada give the plainest express authority in this region. Utah's statute is the most demanding, and Wyoming has none.

### Texas

**Authority.** Local Gov't Code § 271.102 lets a local government join a cooperative purchasing program with a local government or local cooperative organization "of this state or another state," and says the purchase "satisfies any state law requiring the local government to seek competitive bids" **[mirror, verified May 2025]** ([§ 271.102](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._local_gov't_code_section_271.102)). Gov't Code § 791.025 does the same for interlocal purchasing **[mirror]** ([§ 791.025](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_791.025)). **Conditions.** An interlocal contract authorized by each governing body. A purchasing cooperative may not be used for architectural or engineering services, and construction-related purchases over $50,000 through one require a written certification about plans and specifications **[mirror]** ([§ 791.011](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._gov't_code_section_791.011)). **Schools.** An interlocal contract is a listed purchasing method under Educ. Code § 44.031(a); for co-op contracts of $25,000 or more the district must document fees and report them annually in open meeting **[mirror]** ([§ 44.0331](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._educ._code_section_44.0331)). **List.** None found. **Thresholds (S.B. 1173, effective September 1, 2025).** Municipalities: competitive procurement above **$100,000**; from $3,000 to $100,000, contact two historically underutilized businesses. Counties: above $100,000. School districts: $100,000 or more in aggregate per 12 months. Construction under ch. 271: above $100,000 ([SB 1173 bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB01173F.htm)). These come from the enrolled bill analysis; the post-amendment code text was not read directly. No quote requirement below the formal threshold was found for counties or schools, an absence not affirmatively verified.

### New Mexico

**Authority.** The Procurement Code covers local public bodies and school districts alike. NMSA 13-1-135 lets a local public body join a cooperative procurement agreement with external procurement units **[mirror, 2025 code]** ([13-1-135](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-13/article-1/section-13-1-135/)); NMSA 13-1-129 allows purchases without sealed bids at federal supply schedule prices or under a state or central-purchasing-office price agreement **[mirror]** ([13-1-129](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-13/article-1/section-13-1-129/)). No statute simply deems a nationally bid contract a substitute for local bidding. **Conditions.** The agreement must be **approved by each party's governing authority and filed with the state purchasing agent**. Whether a nonprofit-run co-op is an "external procurement unit" was not confirmed. **Schools.** Same statutes. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Direct purchase order up to **$20,000**; small-purchase rules up to **$60,000**; sealed bids or proposals above **[mirror]** ([13-1-125](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-mexico/chapter-13/article-1/section-13-1-125/)). The quote count in the middle band is set by local rules **[unverified]**. Bills to raise the limit to $100,000 died in 2025 and 2026 ([SB 153](https://www.nmlegis.gov/Legislation/Legislation?chamber=S&legType=B&legNo=153&year=26)).

### Arizona

**Authority.** A.R.S. § 41-2632 lets any public procurement unit, which includes political subdivisions, join cooperative purchasing agreements with other units or out-of-state "external procurement activities," and buy under their vendor contracts without separate bidding; nonprofit-administered cooperatives are allowed ([§ 41-2632](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/41/02632.htm)). **Conditions.** Each unit remains responsible for compliance with the laws applicable to its own purchase; construction and professional services must follow the state qualification statutes. **Schools.** A.A.C. R7-2-1191 gives parallel authority, with a duty of **district due diligence** on compliance with the school procurement rules ([R7-2-1191](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R7-2-1191)); award rationale through a school purchasing cooperative must be disclosed ([§ 15-213](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/15/00213.htm)). **List.** None; entities qualify by definition. **Thresholds.** Schools: sealed bids or proposals above **$100,000** (the § 41-2535 amount); the Auditor General's June 2026 update set the minimum written-quote threshold at **$15,000** **[secondary: agency slide deck; full tiers not read]** ([AZ Auditor General](https://www.azauditor.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/AASBO-ASA%20Webinar%20-%20June%202026%20USFR%20slides_0.pdf)). Counties: sealed bids above **$10,000**, or above $100,000 if the board has adopted that amount ([§ 11-254.01](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/11/00254-01.htm)). Public works: indexed base amounts whose 2026 values were not retrieved **[unverified]**. Cities and towns: set by charter or code (inference).

### Colorado

**Authority.** C.R.S. § 24-110-201 lets public procurement units join cooperative purchasing agreements with external procurement units, and subsection (5) lets a local unit participate as permitted by the Procurement Code and by **its own ordinances and rules** **[mirror; code year not shown]** ([§ 24-110-201](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_24-110-201)). The statute enables rather than prescribes; the local charter, ordinance or board policy decides. Subsection (5) as summarized covers supplies or services, not construction, a difference to check against official text. **Schools.** Boards must adopt their own competitive-bidding procedures; school coverage under the cooperative statute is an inference **[mirror]** ([§ 22-32-109](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_22-32-109)). **List.** None; state price agreements are open to local governments **[secondary]** ([DLG](https://dlg.colorado.gov/local-government-purchasing-assistance)). **Thresholds.** Statutory cities: public-improvement construction of **$5,000 or more** by open bidding **[mirror]** ([§ 31-15-712](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_31-15-712)). Special districts: published notice for construction of **$120,000 or more** **[mirror]** ([§ 32-1-1001](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_32-1-1001)). County thresholds are **[unverified]**; the county statute could not be retrieved. Goods and services: local policy.

### Utah

**Authority.** Utah Code § 63G-6a-2105 lets a procurement unit buy under a cooperative purchasing organization's contract **[mirror; currency not guaranteed]** ([§ 63G-6a-2105](https://codes.findlaw.com/ut/title-63g-general-government/ut-code-sect-63g-6a-2105/)). The code applies to counties and municipalities unless they adopt their own procurement code ([§ 63G-6a-103](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title63g/Chapter6a/C63G-6a-S103_2025050720250507.pdf)). **Conditions.** The lead procurement must accord with the Utah code, the solicitation must **identify each party that may purchase**, and each party must sign a **participating addendum**. A generic national contract may or may not meet the identification test. Federal schedule purchases need a written state determination. State cooperative contracts need no addendum. **Schools.** The code applies directly to school districts. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Small-purchase limits are set by each unit's rulemaking authority; the statute has no dollar figure **[mirror]**. Local construction is triggered by an indexed bid limit (2003 bases of $40,000 for building improvements and $125,000 for public works); the 2026 values were not retrieved **[unverified]**. The former $80,000 school-construction trigger was reenacted by 2026 S.B. 164 and should not be assumed to survive ([S.B. 164](https://le.utah.gov/Session/2026/bills/enrolled/SB0164.pdf)).

### Nevada

**Authority.** NRS 332.195 lets a governing body join or use a contract of a governmental entity inside or outside Nevada, the State, another state, or a cooperative purchasing organization, with the vendor's authorization ([NRS ch. 332](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Legal/LawLibrary/NRS/NRS-332.html)). **Conditions.** Vendor authorization. The route is **barred if any portion of the contract requires a Nevada contractor's license**, which removes installed construction. No advertising, savings finding or contract-age limit applies. **Schools.** School districts are "local governments" under the chapter. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Goods and services: no prescribed competition at **$50,000 or less**; over $50,000 to **$100,000**, solicit two or more responses; over $100,000, advertise. Public works: advertise above $100,000; three licensed-contractor bids from $25,000 **[mirror; currency not guaranteed]** ([NRS 338.13862](https://codes.findlaw.com/nv/title-28-public-works-and-planning/nv-rev-st-338-13862/)).

### Wyoming

**Authority.** No express local piggyback statute was found. Participation rests on the general cooperation statute, W.S. 16-1-101, which covers cooperation with other states' entities but does not mention purchasing **[secondary: vendor flyer]** ([Inceptia Wyoming flyer](https://www.inceptia.org/PDF/VG/Cooperative%20Purchasing_Wyoming_021920.pdf)). The municipal bid statute has no cooperative exemption **[mirror, 2025 code]** ([W.S. 15-1-113](https://law.justia.com/codes/wyoming/title-15/chapter-1/article-1/section-15-1-113/)). **Schools.** W.S. 21-3-110(a)(viii) says a district "shall independently meet the requirements" of the bidding paragraph even when buying through a cooperative services board, so a co-op contract does not replace the district's own bids (read through a legislative staff memo, August 2025) ([LSO memo](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2025/SSF-2025082009-01_SchoolFacilitiesProcurementRequirements.pdf)). **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Cities and towns: advertised bids for public improvements above **$75,000**; new automobiles and trucks must be advertised regardless of cost. School supplies and materials: competitive bids over **$25,000**; published call for bids at **$50,000**. School capital construction: published bids over $50,000. County thresholds were **not found**; 2026 session changes were not checked.

## West and Pacific: Hawaii bans after-the-fact piggybacking

Idaho, Oregon and Washington have clear statutory routes; California's is clear only for schools; Hawaii requires participation before the lead contract is awarded.

### California

**Authority.** No single express piggyback statute for cities, counties or special districts was located. Gov. Code § 54202 tells every local agency to adopt its own purchasing policies, so cooperative use rests on the local ordinance or charter **[mirror]** ([§ 54202](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ca/GOV/54202.)). State contracts and cooperative agreements are usable without further bidding under PCC § 10299 **[mirror]** ([§ 10299](https://california.public.law/codes/public_contract_code_section_10299)). **Schools.** PCC § 20118 lets a governing board, without advertising for bids, buy personal property through the contract of any public corporation or agency if the **board determines it is in the district's best interests** **[mirror, marked current Nov. 2, 2026]** ([§ 20118](https://california.public.law/codes/public_contract_code_section_20118)). The text is silent on out-of-state lead agencies and no authority on that point was read. The state education department advises confirming the contract was competitively solicited ([CDE](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ls/fa/bidding.asp)). A 2006 attorney general opinion bars piggybacking for modular buildings on permanent foundations **[secondary]** ([CSBA](https://blog.csba.org/piggyback-contracting/)). **List.** None; the state multiple award schedule program is the state vehicle. **Thresholds.** Schools: formal bids above **$119,100** for equipment, materials, supplies and non-construction services (effective January 1, 2026); public works above **$15,000**; no state quote tier. Agencies that opted into the uniform construction cost accounting act: **$75,000** or less negotiated; up to **$220,000** informal bids; above, formal **[mirror]** ([PCC § 22032](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ca/PCC/22032.)). General-law cities: public projects over **$5,000** **[mirror]** ([PCC § 20162](https://clearlake.municipal.codes/CA/PCC/20162)). City and county goods: local policy. County public-works thresholds were not read **[unverified]**.

### Oregon

**Authority.** ORS 279A.205 authorizes joint, permissive and interstate cooperative procurements, the latter two for goods and services "but not public improvements" **[mirror]** ([ORS 279A.205](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_279a.205)). ORS 279A.220 covers national and out-of-state cooperatives **[mirror, as of Jan. 1, 2023]** ([ORS 279A.220](https://codes.findlaw.com/or/title-26-public-facilities-contracting-and-insurance/or-rev-st-sect-279a-220/)). **Conditions.** The administering agency used an open and impartial competitive process substantially equivalent to Oregon's; the solicitation and contract allow other agencies to use them; the contractor agrees. For interstate use the buyer must either have been listed in a solicitation advertised in Oregon or publish a notice of intent with a 7-day comment window, and make a written best-interest determination if comments arrive. The interstate statute has no dollar trigger; the **$250,000** notice trigger belongs to permissive (in-state) cooperatives **[mirror]** ([ORS 279A.215](https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_279a.215)). **Schools.** No separate statute; coverage under the same code is an inference not verified against the definition. **List.** None. **Thresholds.** Goods and services: **$25,000 or less**, any practical manner; over $25,000 to **$250,000**, three informal quotes; above, sealed bids or proposals ([ORS ch. 279B](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors279B.html)). Public improvements: under $25,000 exempt; quotes to $100,000; bidding above ([ORS ch. 279C](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors279c.html)). The official pages carry a banner that some sections were amended in the 2026 session; which ones was not determined **[currency unconfirmed]**.

### Washington

**Authority.** RCW 39.34.030 (Interlocal Cooperation Act) is the piggyback statute. Under the State Auditor's reading, a public agency's bid obligations are satisfied when the awarding public agency complied with its own statutory requirements and posted the solicitation online; the statute text itself could not be loaded, so this rests on agency guidance ([SAO guide](https://www.sao.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-05/Piggybacking-under-Washington-state-law.pdf)). The host agency may be anywhere in the United States ([MRSC](https://mrsc.org/Explore-Topics/procurement/basics/Piggybacking)). **Conditions.** A **signed interlocal agreement** before use; the lead agency followed its own bid law; the awarding entity is a public agency, a point to verify for cooperatives. A co-op membership agreement may serve as the interlocal agreement. Public works piggybacking is legally possible but rare. **Schools.** Same authority (inference). **List.** None; the Auditor does not pre-approve cooperatives. **Thresholds.** School districts: under **$40,000** no state requirement; $40,000 to **$75,000**, three quotes; over $75,000, sealed bids; public works formal at $100,000 ([RCW 28A.335.190](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/Law/RCWPDF/RCW%20%2028A%20TITLE/RCW%20%2028A.335%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2028A.335%20.190.pdf)). A 2026 bill to raise these appears not to have passed. Counties: sealed bids at **$50,000**, with a vendor-list process from $10,000 ([RCW 36.32.245](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/RCW/RCW%20%2036%20%20TITLE/RCW%20%2036%20.%2032%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2036%20.%2032%20.245.htm)). Cities: public works bids over $75,500 (single craft) or $150,000 (multiple); purchases over $40,000 for smaller cities and towns; larger cities set their own ([MRSC City Bidding Book](https://mrsc.org/explore-topics/procurement/basics/city-bidding-book)). The small works roster limit is $350,000 through December 31, 2026, then $530,000.

### Idaho

**Authority.** Idaho Code § 67-2807 lets a political subdivision, **with governing-board approval**, join cooperative purchasing agreements with the state, other political subdivisions, other government entities or associations of them, and cooperative programs of any association offering goods or services "as a result of competitive solicitation processes"; items so obtained are deemed acquired in compliance ([§ 67-2807](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title67/T67CH28/SECT67-2807)). **Conditions.** Board approval and a competitive solicitation. ### Hawaii

**Authority.** HRS § 103D-802 lets a public procurement unit join cooperative purchasing agreements with external procurement units under policy-board rules ([§ 103D-802](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol02_Ch0046-0115/HRS0103D/HRS_0103D-0802.htm)). That counties fall under the state procurement code, and that the single statewide education department procures under it, are **[unverified against statute text]**. **Conditions.** The rules require **prior written approval of the chief procurement officer**, naming the Hawaii unit as a participant in the external solicitation, and public notice in Hawaii; they prohibit piggybacking on an existing cooperative agreement "without prior public notice and intent to participate" ([HAR § 3-128-2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/hawaii/Haw-Code-R-SS-3-128-2)). A Hawaii agency must join before award. **List.** None published; approval is case by case. **Thresholds.** Small-purchase procedures apply to goods or services under **$100,000** and construction under **$250,000**; from $50,000 an electronic system is required ([§ 103D-305](https://data.capitol.hawaii.gov/hrscurrent/Vol02_Ch0046-0115/HRS0103D/HRS_0103D-0305.htm)). ## Conclusion

The practical test for a national cooperative contract is narrower than the question "does this state allow piggybacking." Four facts about the specific contract decide most states: whether a public agency, not a nonprofit administrator, awarded it; whether the solicitation was advertised and named or opened itself to other governments; how old it is (North Carolina, South Dakota and Louisiana set 12-month windows); and whether any part of the purchase is construction. A contract that passes all four is usable in most of the country with a resolution or membership agreement. One that fails the public-lead-agency test is exposed in New York, New Jersey, Tennessee and Washington at a minimum, and one that bundles installation or construction is exposed almost everywhere.

Two cautions follow for anyone building on this survey. First, thresholds are moving faster than the mirrors that publish them: at least a dozen states changed a figure in 2025 or 2026, several index annually, and the 2026 values for Ohio, Arkansas schools, Rhode Island, Utah and Arizona public works were not found. Second, the states where authority looked weakest (Rhode Island, Vermont schools, Wyoming schools, Illinois, Louisiana before 2026, and the interlocal-only states) are also the ones where the notes relied most on vendor flyers and inference. Those entries, and every flagged figure, warrant a direct read of the official code before they are treated as settled.
